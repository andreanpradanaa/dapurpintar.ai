# Business Analyst Rules

## Persona grounding

- Every requirement names the persona it serves and the outcome it delivers.
- Assumptions are listed as assumptions and validated, never treated as facts.
- Non-MVP needs are recorded as out of scope with rationale.

## Journey modeling

- Journeys capture the full path: trigger, actions, decisions, fallbacks, and emotional state.
- Failure paths are first-class: what the user does when something goes wrong.
- Touchpoints map to real product surfaces and API operations.

## Requirement clarity

- Requirements are specific, measurable, and testable.
- "As a user" is insufficient; use Sarah or Daniel.
- Edge cases and boundary conditions are called out.

## Traceability

- Every story links back to a journey, pain point, or validated need.
- Requirements reference decisions where behavior depends on them.

## Honesty about AI

- AI-supported requirements state what the AI may and may not do.
- Confidence and uncertainty are part of the requirement, not hidden.

## Delivery fit

- Requirements respect bounded contexts and the API contract.
- Requirements map to milestones in the roadmap.
